The issue of the expansion of internet shopping has had a severe impact on various sectors, and the gaming industry is no exception. It is easy to see the dissatisfaction of the consumers in respect of the problem of commercializing constant flow of poor-quality goods and service, in games. Often especially as a player, I can see lots of in-game purchases in the form of currency or Items that players can buy in the game to improve their characters. Sometimes the content gets wasted either with the story being very wonderful or scenarios blocked too deep in down the in-game purchases levels. There are paywalls and loot boxes in video games, and those can be fine, but only if the game is inherently free and players get the value of whatever money they spend. However, in fairness it should be mentioned that such immoral practices are not welcome, because they disrupt the game and players of every kind.

It is difficult to imagine a world devoid of video gaming and associated platforms with the kind of history on Steam. It is worth noting how such platforms make custom games more awesome for the creators and people who work in the video game sector while at the same time, cloud sourcing nurtures rip offs. For instance, while a specific game is popular most cheap clones increase aggressively affecting the profit margin of the main product owners and most importantly the thrill of players. All these aspects accentuate both the pros and cons of the game mass-production technique in the gaming industry.

My personal experiences have also influenced the way I view the subject. I remember buying a digital item in a game for more money than I should have, and missing it, how this experience taught me to be more critical when buying things online. While I appreciate the microtransaction and freemium business models, I do not agree with their application when it does not benefit gameplay or the enjoyment of others in the game. It is also worth noting that the low-quality products don’t just hurt the Arm-Standing consumers, but also the tiny game development companies; this is especially the case when the market is infiltrated with copycat games.

In extreme cases it is understood that Consumer reviews and the community can affect the credibility of the game design, yet in many cases the overwhelming feeling is a negative one, forums and review sites are usually not credible as this is a very common issue if rival companies “boost” the product with fake reviews. In contrast to this, evaluations help developers build better games by looking into issues with the games from the viewpoint of the people playing them. And these also give a platform to the ever-popular game designers to acquire some beautiful creations which are high quality as the quality of the goods offered for that purpose may not be the best for the specific framework of computer games.

As we reach this conclusion game designers should always focus on the quality rather than the quantity. A saying that comes from Korea states that ‘if it is still standing, then it is strong’. This saying proves that for one to beat competition in certain industries, they must learn to have only the best products. By examining the issues related to people’s bad buying experiences that were poor quality or expensive, game developers will be able to make sure that their games have good packaging and that they can be easily sold and accepted in the market
